## 1. Beginners & First Purchase

**1.** First three questions to a total beginner. **Alan:** Establish where they'll play most (CQB, woodland or both), total budget incl. safety gear and power, and what matters most to them — reliability, realism, weight or upgrade potential. Those narrow the choice better than appearance.

**2.** Rifle you hand a first-timer most often. **Draft (research):** The usual first-gun picks are the G&G Combat Machine CM16 line (Raider 2.0, ARP9) or a Specna Arms CORE/EDGE M4 — reliable out of the box, standard M4/V2 platform so spares and mags are easy, and they hold value. Cheaper heavily-styled replicas often use proprietary parts/weaker hop units. Confirm Alan's specific pick.

**3.** Realistic all-in starting budget. **Alan:** Roughly **€300–€450** for a dependable entry AEG, battery+charger, quality BBs and rated eye pro. Don't cut corners on eye protection and the charger; cheap BBs/batteries are false economies (feeding problems, poor performance, early failure).

**4.** Cheap first guns you refuse to sell / steer from. **Needs Alan** (his shop policy/opinion).

**5.** AEG vs gas vs spring for a first buy. **Alan:** For most beginners an **AEG is the safest first primary** — consistent, easy, economical, less cold-affected. Gas suits players who value realism and accept maintenance/weather sensitivity. Spring is limited as a primary except sniper/shotgun roles. Common myth: gas or a sniper is automatically more powerful/effective — reliability, hop setup and consistency matter far more.

**6.** Most common battery mistake. **Alan:** Treating a LiPo like a NiMH — wrong charger, running it flat, or charging unattended. That can permanently damage the battery, swell it, damage wiring/electronics, and at worst cause a fire.

**7.** LiPo vs NiMH for a first battery. **Alan:** A **7.4V LiPo with a proper balance charger** is usually the best balance of performance and simplicity where the gun suits it. NiMH is more forgiving but bulkier with slower trigger response. Shape, connector, space, wiring, fuse and any MOSFET all matter. An 11.1V LiPo isn't a default beginner battery unless the gun is built for it.

**8.** BB weight/quality beginners get wrong. **Alan:** For a stock AEG, good-quality **0.25g** is a sensible start. Cheap/seamed/poorly-polished BBs misfeed, jam or damage the hop. Too light drifts and exaggerates inconsistency; too heavy for the hop may drop early or feed poorly.

**9.** Gear beyond the gun people forget. **Alan:** Full-seal rated eye protection, lower-face/dental protection, supportive grippy footwear, the correct battery+charger, quality BBs, water, and a workable spare-mag plan. Check site-specific requirements before game day.

**10.** FPS/joule limits Dublin sites enforce + chrono catch-outs. **Needs Alan + verify with each site.**

**11.** Age/ID/purchase requirements incl. NI deliveries. **Needs Alan + legal.**

**12.** Upgrade/accessory beginners rush but shouldn't. **Alan:** Internal performance parts, a stronger spring, an 11.1V battery, or an expensive optic before they understand the stock gun. Play several games, find a real limitation, then upgrade the part that addresses it.

**13.** Brands with best/worst spares support in Ireland. **Draft (research):** Standard V2/V3 + M4/AK platforms (G&G, Specna Arms, Cyma; Tokyo Marui as the cloned spec) give the best parts interchangeability and support. Proprietary-gearbox or heavily-licensed designs (some Ares/Amoeba EFCS, PTS, certain licensed replicas) can mean waiting on specific spares. What's quick to source locally depends on distributors — confirm with Alan.

**14.** Reliability of entry Specna EDGE / G&G Combat Machine tier. **Draft (research):** Reliable for regular skirmish; rarely fail catastrophically early. First to go are consumable/stress parts — hop rubber and nub (fading range), the battery over time, occasionally grip-motor connections or the plastic selector plate. Gearbox internals typically last a long season+ with decent care. Treat any specific round-count as confirm-with-Alan.

**15.** Rent first vs buy first. **Alan:** Rent first when someone's unsure they'll enjoy it, hasn't handled different platforms, or doesn't know CQB vs woodland. One or two rental days prevent buying the wrong length, weight, control layout or loadout.

**16.** First thing to do to a new gun before its first game. **Alan:** Charge and inspect the battery, confirm it feeds, clean the inner barrel with a dry rod, set the hop with the BB weight you'll actually use, and chrono it if possible. Don't start by opening the gearbox or adding upgrades.

**17.** Talking a beginner out of a sniper first. **Alan:** Poor first primary for most — low rate of fire, less forgiving, often needs expensive tuning, may face minimum-engagement rules. Beginners get more effective range and playing time from a reliable AEG while learning movement, positioning and hop.

**18.** In-store advantage over a UK ship-in. **Alan:** Handle the gun, check size/controls, match the correct battery and accessories, test feeding/function, and get local aftercare. A remote order can't fit or troubleshoot the same way.

**19.** Most common "broke day one" warranty claim — fault vs user error. **Needs Alan** (shop experience).

**20.** One piece of advice to save a first-year buyer most money/frustration. **Alan:** Buy a reliable common-platform AEG and spend the rest on rated eye pro, a proper charger, quality BBs and footwear. Don't chase max FPS or replace working internals before the gun shows a real weakness.

## 2. Product & Brand Expertise

**1.** First AEG at €150–200. **Draft (research):** Sensible picks: G&G CM16 Raider 2.0 / CM16 range, G&G ARP9 (CQB), Specna Arms CORE/EDGE M4/M16. All on the standard V2 gearbox with good hop units and easy spares. A Cyma M4/AK is a strong slightly-cheaper option. Confirm current stock/pricing with Alan.

**2.** Specna EDGE vs G&G Combat Machine. **Draft (research):** Close — same core platform. G&G tends to win on out-of-the-box polish/finish (and ETU/MOSFET on some models); Specna EDGE tends to win on value-for-features (electronic trigger/MOSFET, quick-change spring, fuller kit) for someone who'll tinker/upgrade. Little between them for pure reliability. Confirm local experience with Alan.

**3.** Brands/models most often in for repair + what fails. **Draft (research):** Market-wide, budget AEGs (entry Cyma/JG/Double Eagle, older Well/Galaxy) and high-mileage guns of any brand. Common failures: hop units (worn/misaligned buckings, cracked chambers), wiring/connectors, motor/trigger-contact wear; shell cracks and stripped teeth more on cheap/over-sprung setups. **This shop's actual bench data — confirm with Alan.**

**4.** Tokyo Marui — where it's worth the premium in Ireland. **Needs Alan** (his positioning). *[General: TM is the reliability/consistency benchmark, especially GBB and NGRS; premium is in QC and internals rather than trims.]*

**5.** GBB pistols that run reliably through an Irish winter. **Needs Alan** *(overlaps 3.8 gas-cold research; his platform picks needed).*

**6.** Green gas vs CO2 for a first pistol. **Alan (S6.4 covers this):** Green gas is easier/simpler for a beginner pistol; CO2 holds pressure better in cold but runs higher pressure. Only use CO2 in a gun/mag designed for it — otherwise it accelerates wear or damages valves/seals/nozzles/slides.

**7.** BB weight by gun type + cheap brands that cause jams. **Draft (research):** Rough guide: 0.20–0.25g for lower-powered pistols/standard AEGs, 0.25–0.28g all-round skirmish, 0.28–0.32g for higher-FPS DMR/support, 0.30–0.45g for bolt snipers. Cheap unbranded "bag of 5000" BBs are the usual jam/misfeed/hop-damage culprits (poor sphericity, seams, loose tolerance). Confirm stocked brands with Alan.

**8.** Bio BB brands that feed cleanly. **Draft (research):** Widely-regarded feeding brands include Geoffs (Bio-Shot), BLS, G&G bio, Nuprol RB, Valken bio. Budget bio BBs suffer wider tolerance, seams/dimples, moisture sensitivity (can go brittle if stored badly) and batch inconsistency. Confirm stocked lines with Alan.

**9.** Upgrade parts that waste money vs cheap high-impact parts. **Alan:** Waste = replacing working internals or fitting a stronger spring with no goal. Best value = quality BBs, clean barrel, correct hop setup, and (where stock parts are poor) a well-fitted bucking and nub. Change internals to solve a measured problem, not because a list says so.

**10.** When a MOSFET earns its place. **Alan:** When the setup draws more current, uses an 11.1V battery, needs electronic trigger functions, or would otherwise place heavy arcing/wear on mechanical contacts. Don't casually pair a basic-contact gun with a high-load spring and high-output battery.

**12.** Krytac vs ICS vs VFC (~€350+). **Draft (research):** Krytac (esp. Trident) — strong out-of-the-box skirmish reliability and QC, a "buy and play" choice. ICS — two-piece split gearbox that's easy to maintain, good value, feel/consistency varies by model. VFC — praised for realism/trademarks/build feel, more enthusiast-oriented and sometimes fussier than Krytac for plug-and-play. Confirm with Alan.

**13.** Acetech tracers + common pairing mistakes. **Draft (research):** Popular picks: Acetech Lighter S (compact, fits many mock suppressors), Blaster/Predator. Mistakes: expecting standard BBs to glow (you need UV-reactive tracer BBs, usually green/red), fouling/over-tightening, and a worn/mismatched bucking causing feeding issues that show as inconsistent tracer performance. Confirm unit-for-gun recs with Alan.

**16.** Nuprol / Vorsk / Valken quality. **Draft (research):** Nuprol (Weekend Warrior house brand) — solid budget-to-mid, best known for good-value BBs, gas, pyro/accessories. Vorsk — value-focused; gas pistols and some AEGs often punch above price, QC more variable. Valken — mid-range; BBs and gear generally well regarded for the price. Confirm items stocked with Alan.

**17.** Common YouTube belief wrong for Irish skirmish. **Alan:** That higher advertised FPS automatically means better range/accuracy/quality. In practice hop performance, air-seal consistency, BB quality, build quality and reliability matter more, especially where power is limited.

## 3. Repairs & Tech

**1.** Diagnostic order for a dead AEG. **Alan:** Confirm safe/unloaded; test battery voltage and a known-good battery; inspect connector/wiring; check the fuse; check motor connections and any MOSFET fault; only then investigate motor and gearbox. Battery, connector and fuse faults are far more common than a destroyed gearbox.

**2.** Common gearbox failures V2 vs V3. **Draft (research):** V2 — shell cracks around the cylinder window and behind the trigger; stock partial-metal pistons and the nozzle/tappet fail early. V3 — sturdier shell, so failures skew to the tappet plate, piston and sector gear stripping the rack, plus the more exposed nozzle/hop interface. Short version: V2 breaks at the shell first, V3 punishes piston/tappet first.

**3.** Full-steel vs half-steel pistons and stripped-piston failures. **Draft (research):** Many stock guns ship a polycarbonate piston with only the last one or two teeth in metal; that concentrates pickup/release stress on those teeth and the softer plastic ones. Under higher ROF, AoE problems or poor pickup, the plastic teeth shear — a stripped piston. A full-steel-tooth (or reinforced) piston spreads the load, which is why upgraders swap when raising ROF/spring rate. Whether a given stock gun needs it — confirm with Alan.

**4.** Most common self-inflicted DIY gearbox damage + fix cost. **Needs Alan** (shop cost/experience). *(General DIY errors covered in 8.5.)*

**5.** What a correctly-set motor sounds like. **Alan:** A smooth, even whir without a sharp squeal or coarse grinding. Too high = tight/strained/high-pitched; too low = rough/gear-like. Leaving it wrong adds heat and accelerates wear on the pinion and bevel gear and can strip teeth.

**6.** Buckings/nubs to bin + combos for 0.25g vs 0.28–0.32g. **Draft (research):** Stock buckings are often replaced for inconsistent/too-soft rubber; common upgrades are flat-hop/R-hop or quality flat/concave buckings (Maple Leaf, Prometheus, Modify) with a matching nub. Stock-FPS on 0.25g: a ~50–60° flat bucking with a flat/H-nub gives clean lift. Stepping to 0.28–0.32g: a flatter/longer contact patch (flat-hop/R-hop) with a firmer/longer nub stays stable at lower FPS. Dial in on the bench — confirm with Alan.

**7.** FPS/joule limits Irish sites enforce (AEG/DMR/bolt). **Needs Alan + verify with each site.**

**8.** Green gas in an Irish winter. **Draft (research):** Green gas is propane at a set pressure; vapour pressure drops with temperature, so you lose pressure — low velocity, sluggish/short blowback, failure to cycle, poor feeding/gas-lock. When it short-strokes or won't cycle, switch to a hotter gas within the gun's rating, warm the mags, or move to AEG/HPA/CO2. Lighter-reciprocating-mass guns and (rated) CO2 versions cope better. Never exceed a mag/gun's rated gas. Confirm suitability with Alan.

**9.** Three most common GBB gas-leak causes + economic vs not. **Alan:** Usual leak points are the fill valve, output valve and base/main mag seal. Dried/damaged O-rings, dirt and loose valves are often cheap fixes. A cracked mag body, damaged casting, corroded valve seat or unavailable proprietary part can make it uneconomical.

**10.** Lubrication — greases/oils by area + silicone-vs-lithium mistake. **Alan:** A small amount of suitable gear grease on gears/contact surfaces, appropriate cylinder lube only where needed, silicone-safe oil/grease on gas seals and O-rings. Keep oil out of the inner barrel and hop rubber. Common mistake: spraying silicone everywhere or letting petroleum-based lube contact rubber — contaminates the hop, attracts grit, damages seals.

**11.** LiPo storage/charging reality. **Alan:** A correctly-sized 7.4V LiPo is usually enough; capacity by battery space/use, discharge rating only needs to meet current demand. Store at **~3.8V/cell**, use a balance charger, inspect for swelling/damage, never leave fully charged or flat for long. Over-discharging kills packs fastest.

**12.** When a MOSFET is worth it + burnt-contact repair cost. **Pending/Needs Alan** (general: see 2.10; the repair cost is shop-specific).

**13.** Custom DMR build requirements + target FPS to stay site-legal. **Pending** (researchable — deferred half).

**14.** Tightbore bore diameter for Irish ranges. **Alan:** A well-made **6.03–6.05mm** barrel is a sensible general range, but bore size alone doesn't create accuracy — straightness, finish, cleanliness, stable air seal, bucking/nub, correct hop and good BBs usually matter more.

**15.** Guns/brands you steer beginners from (proprietary/hard parts). **Needs Alan** (shop opinion).

**16.** Player self-maintenance vs leave-to-tech + service interval. **Alan:** After a game: remove battery and mag, clear the chamber safely, wipe the exterior, dry moisture, clean the inner barrel with a dry non-abrasive patch. Store dry and ventilated. Don't open the gearbox, alter shimming or lube the hop rubber unless you know exactly what you're doing. For a gun used ~twice a month, inspection/service every **6–12 months** or sooner if symptoms appear.

**17.** Realistic repair turnaround times + current wait. **Needs Alan** (shop times).

**18.** Parts you stock vs order in; longest-down failure. **Needs Alan** (shop stock).

**19.** Where upgrade money makes a difference for a mid-level player. **Alan:** First: quality BBs, hop consistency, bucking/nub and fixing air-seal inconsistency. Motor/electronics for trigger response, efficiency or control features. Avoid a stronger spring or extremely tight barrel that adds stress without usable accuracy.

**20.** Telltale signs a gearbox is about to fail. **Alan:** New grinding/screeching/clicking; a sudden drop or fluctuation in ROF; excessive motor/grip heat; intermittent firing; electrical smell; repeated lock-ups; double-firing; worsening misfeeds. Stop when the sound changes sharply — continuing can turn a small fault into a full rebuild.

## 6. Buying Decisions

**1.** Beginner AEG €150–250 to steer toward / away from. **Draft (research):** Toward a G&G CM16 (Raider 2.0/ARP9) or Specna EDGE M4 — reliable, standard-platform, easy parts/mags. Away from cheap heavily-marketed no-brand or "gel/AEG hybrid" replicas (proprietary/low-quality gearboxes and hop units — unreliable, hard to repair/upgrade). Confirm current model/pricing with Alan.

**2.** Case against a GBB first + who a GBB first suits. **Alan:** Generally a poor first primary — expensive mags, lower capacity, temperature-sensitive gas, more cleaning/lube/seal maintenance. It suits someone who knowingly prioritises recoil, handling and realism over simplicity and all-weather consistency.

**3.** How gas falls off in Irish cold + temperature. **Draft (research):** Green gas loses pressure as temperature drops — weak/short blowback, low FPS, poor cycling. It begins to fall off noticeably below ~15°C and becomes genuinely unreliable around ~10°C and below, worse near freezing. Varies by gas/gun/how recently the mag was handled — ballpark, not hard cut-offs.

**4.** Green gas vs CO2 for a first gas pistol. **Alan:** Green gas is easier/simpler for a beginner pistol; CO2 holds pressure better in cold but runs higher pressure. Only use CO2 in a gun and mag designed for it — otherwise it accelerates wear or damages valves/seals/nozzles/slides.

**5.** FPS sites enforce + DMR/single-shot joule catch-outs. **Needs Alan + verify with each site.**

**6.** Why joules-on-a-set-weight beats chasing FPS. **Alan:** FPS is speed only; joules is actual kinetic energy and accounts for BB weight. A gun can show lower FPS with a heavier BB while producing similar/greater energy. Chrono to the site's stated method and BB weight, and be aware of joule creep rather than relying on an FPS number.

**7.** Default BB weight for a stock 350–400 FPS AEG + 0.20/0.25/0.28g difference. **Draft (research):** 0.25g or 0.28g is the common default; 0.20g suits lower-powered guns/plinking. 0.20→0.25g noticeably improves stability/range (carries energy, resists wind); 0.25→0.28g refines it (flatter, more consistent). Trade-off is slightly lower velocity as weight rises, but accuracy/range improve up to where the hop can no longer lift the BB.

**8.** Damage cheap BBs cause + trusted brands. **Draft (research):** Cheap/seamed/out-of-tolerance BBs score or wear the hop rubber and inner barrel; seams/oversize shave material, cause inconsistent hop and jams/fliers; a shattered BB fouls the barrel/hop. Widely-trusted: Geoffs, BLS, G&G, Nuprol, Valken, Madbull. A quality correctly-sized BB is one of the cheapest ways to protect internals.

**9.** When bio BBs are genuinely needed. **Alan:** When a site or landowner specifies them. A good bio BB should perform much like a good standard BB — the key factors are consistent diameter, polish, roundness and storage away from heat/moisture. Cheap bio BBs suffer the same feeding/consistency problems as any poor ammo.

**10.** Matching BB weight to gun power (pistol/rifle/sniper). **Draft (research):** Higher power benefits from heavier BBs: ~300 FPS pistol on 0.20–0.25g, ~400 FPS rifle on 0.25–0.32g, ~500 FPS sniper often 0.36–0.45g. Common mistakes: very light 0.20g in a high-FPS gun (unstable, wind-sensitive) or over-weighting a low-powered gun so the hop can't lift it. Check site FPS/energy limits first.

**11.** LiPo vs NiMH + honest LiPo risk + C-rating that matters. **Alan:** A 7.4V LiPo is usually the practical performance choice for a compatible stock AEG; NiMH is more tolerant but bulkier/less responsive. LiPo risk is mainly mishandling (wrong charger, damage, short circuit, over-discharge, unattended charging). Capacity must fit the stock and game length; C-rating only needs to supply the current the gun demands — a higher number doesn't automatically help.

**12.** Battery mistakes that bite + cost. **Pending** (general covered in 1.6/3.11; cost is shop-specific).

**13.** Deans vs Tamiya + does a MOSFET change the battery. **Alan:** Deans-style connectors give a firmer connection and lower resistance than small Tamiya, but conversion is only worthwhile if soldered correctly and both gun and batteries are standardised. A MOSFET can protect trigger contacts and add functions but doesn't automatically mean running an 11.1V battery.

**14.** Most common "bought online, now broken" fault. **Needs Alan** (shop experience).

**15.** Brands that hold up vs ones you avoid stocking. **Needs Alan** (shop opinion; general reliability in 2.3).

**16.** Better gun vs better BBs/battery/hop — where money matters most. **Alan:** Start with a dependable base gun. After that, quality BBs, a correctly matched battery/charger and a good hop setup improve the real experience more than cosmetic parts or random gearbox upgrades. Expensive parts can't fully compensate for a poor or incompatible base platform.

**17.** What UK ship-in shops get customers wrong on. **Draft (research):** Gas and gas mags are the big one — carriers/shops often won't ship pressurised gas (and often loose batteries) internationally, so orders arrive incomplete. Voltage/battery fit — customers order a pack that won't fit the stock or the wrong chemistry/charger. Warranty/returns are harder across a border (return postage, refunds, repair turnaround). Buying locally gets the right compatible battery/charger in person, gas/consumables that can't be posted, and local warranty/repair. Keep any import/energy-limit specifics as "check current requirements", not asserted.

**18.** Gas/CO2 shipping and storage — what can't be posted. **Needs Alan + verify carriage rules.**

**19.** Realistic order of FPS upgrades + where people waste money. **Alan:** First establish the legal/site energy limit and measure the gun consistently. Correct air seal, hop and BB choice before power. Change the spring only when a measured adjustment is needed, then chrono again to the site's method. Chasing FPS without improving consistency/hop adds stress without useful range.

## 7. Gear & Loadouts

**1.** First €150 of gear (not the gun), in priority order. **Alan:** Rated full-seal eye protection, lower-face/dental protection, supportive grippy boots, gloves, anti-fog and hydration, then a simple way to carry magazines. A basic chest rig or belt and one or two spare mags beat a helmet, radio, sidearm or heavy plate carrier at the start.

**2.** Eye protection that resists fogging in Irish damp. **Draft (research):** Sealed thermal (dual-pane) goggles resist fogging best (the trapped air layer keeps the inner lens near skin temperature); active fans and quality anti-fog coatings/sprays help further. Mesh is not advisable for eyes — a close/fragmenting BB can drive material through the gaps and it doesn't stop ricochet fragments; full sealed polycarbonate is the safer standard. Confirm stocked models with Alan.

**3.** Single most common first-day gear mistake. **Pending** (researchable — deferred half).

**4.** Chest rig vs plate carrier vs battle belt for woodland. **Alan:** A chest rig is the best general start (light, ventilated, carries essentials); a battle belt suits a very light load or CQB; a plate carrier only when the player specifically wants its fit/protection/load distribution. It's the wrong choice when it adds heat, bulk and weight without carrying what the player needs.

**5.** Boots for wet muddy Irish woodland. **Draft (research):** What matters: solid ankle support (boggy ground rolls ankles), an aggressive lugged sole for mud grip, and a sensible waterproofing choice (a breathable membrane for rain/dew, though deep bog floods over the top — some prefer boots that drain and dry). Fit and being broken-in outweigh "tactical" styling or ultralight racing soles.

**6.** How many mags + placement for fast reloads. **Alan:** Around **three to six mid-caps** depending on capacity, game length and reload availability. Put primary mags on the support-hand side within natural reach; mirror for a left-handed player. Keep the rig front clear enough to crouch and go prone.

**7.** CQB vs woodland loadout differences beyond camo. **Alan:** CQB favours a shorter gun, low-profile rig, fewer bulky pouches, secure retention and a sling that keeps the gun close. Woodland carries more ammo, water and layers and a supportive two-point sling. Mid-caps are quieter/more consistent; hi-caps carry more but rattle and need winding.

**8.** Camo patterns for Irish woodland/hedgerow. **Draft (research):** Greener multi-tone patterns blend well (British DPM, MTP/Multicam Tropic, general "woodland" greens) against damp foliage; arid/tan-heavy patterns (Multicam Arid, three-colour desert, coyote solids) stand out badly; plain black reads as an unnatural silhouette in daylight. Breaking up outline with local foliage matters as much as the printed pattern.

**9.** Two-point vs single-point sling. **Alan:** A two-point adjustable sling is the best all-round choice — supports the gun and tightens to the body. A single-point feels quick in CQB but swings more freely. Poor adjustment is what causes the muzzle/stock to hit knees, snag gear or drag when kneeling.

**10.** Gloves/knee pads/lower-face protection people skip. **Draft (research):** Beginners skip all three and regret it: bare hands get BB welts and bramble cuts and stiffen in the cold; unprotected knees bruise/graze on wet rock and roots; skipping lower-face/mouth protection risks chipped teeth and split lips from close hits (many treat dental protection as non-negotiable). Cheap gloves, basic knee pads and a mouth guard prevent the injuries that most end a beginner's day.

**11.** Durable gear brands vs cheap components that fail. **Draft (research):** Durability is in the details — bar-tacked stress points, high-denier Cordura, box-stitched webbing, reputable hardware. Fastest failures: brittle plastic buckles (crack in cold), low-grade zips (jam/split with grit/mud), weak elastic/velcro mag retention (mags bounce out). Check stitching and buckles first, not pouch count. Confirm stocked brands with Alan.

**12.** Smartest layering for a full Irish woodland day. **Alan:** Moisture-wicking base layer, a light insulating mid-layer when needed, and a breathable waterproof outer that can be vented/removed. Avoid heavy cotton next to skin (stays wet/cold). Carry one compact dry/warm layer rather than wearing everything from the start.

**13.** Hydration and load-carrying without fatigue. **Alan:** A bottle or bladder — whichever the player will actually clean and drink from. Carry only the water/ammo/gear needed for that game, keep weight close to the body, and remove duplicate tools, oversized pouches and unnecessary armour that cause fatigue by lunch.

**14.** Gear people over-buy. **Alan:** Plate carriers, helmets, radios, sidearms, optics and excessive pouches. Better spent on eye protection, boots, anti-fog, quality BBs, a spare battery and a comfortable simple rig.

**15.** Indoor CQB loadout specifics. **Alan:** Keep it compact — shorter primary, low-profile rig/belt, fewer mags, secure pouches, lower-profile face protection, and a sling that controls the gun tightly. Avoid loose straps, oversized utility pouches and anything that catches on doorways.

**16.** Dump pouches and mid-cap recovery. **Alan:** Place a dump pouch where the support hand reaches without looking (usually behind the hip on that side). Practise removing a mag, dropping it straight into the pouch and seating the replacement. Keep it structured enough to find but closed during movement so mags don't bounce out.

**17.** Full-seal vs mesh vs goggles; under-16 vs adult requirements. **Needs Alan + verify site rules** (his policy; eye/dental protection is safety-critical — general guidance in 7.2/7.10).

**18.** Sizing/fit mistakes ordering rigs/plate carriers online. **Alan:** Measure the chest and waist where the rig will sit, check the maker's adjustment range, and allow for layers underneath. For a plate carrier check torso length so it doesn't sit on the stomach or clash with the belt. Buyers often size from clothing labels instead of the product's actual measurements.
