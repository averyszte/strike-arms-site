## READ THIS FIRST — corrections that invalidate earlier work

Pass 2 corrects four things the site and its drafts were built on. These are not minor.

### 1. The gun range is ASG, CYMA and EVOLUTION

Pass 1 built its first-gun recommendations around **G&G Combat Machine** and **Specna Arms
EDGE**. Pass 2 states plainly that **Strike Arms does not sell either brand**.

Consequences:
- Every pass-1 product pick is void. They are not just unconfirmed, they are wrong shop.
- The 15-brand list in the Brands hub comes from **mock catalogue data**, not real stock. It needs
  replacing with the real range before any per-brand page is built.
- Specna and G&G may still be referenced as *neutral comparison* brands. They must never be the
  centre of a Strike Arms recommendation.
- The three-tier house answer pass 2 proposes: **CYMA** (beginner workhorse), **EVOLUTION**
  M4/Ghost-type (modern mid-tier), **ASG CZ Scorpion EVO** (premium compact). Exact live-stock
  SKUs still need Alan. [10][11][12]

### 2. Ireland's power limit is 1 joule, and it applies to every class

Pass 2: muzzle energy **above 1 J** makes the device a firearm under Irish law. [1] Irish sites
enforce below 1 J for **AEG, DMR and bolt-action alike** — there is **no higher Irish DMR or
sniper tier** the way UK sites operate. [1][6]

Consequences:
- Any UK-derived figure (400 fps rifle, 500 fps sniper, 0.30–0.45 g sniper BBs) is wrong for an
  Irish page. Pass 2 says so explicitly at §6.10.
- ~328 fps is *only* the 1 J equivalent **on a 0.20 g BB**. Quoting a bare FPS number is
  meaningless without the test weight.
- **This is a legal claim and is NOT yet published anywhere on the site.** See the publication
  warning at the end.

### 3. Irish purchase age is under-16 for the dealer prohibition, not 18

Selling a *realistic imitation firearm* to a person **under 16** is an offence. [2] A shop may
impose a stricter 18+ policy — Strike Arms must state which it actually operates. Northern
Ireland is UK jurisdiction: 18+, and a realistic black RIF normally needs a recognised statutory
defence. [4][5]

### 4. Two-tone is a UK sales-law concept, not a general Irish requirement

A full-black sub-1 J replica can be sold in Ireland by a registered dealer if otherwise
compliant. [2][4][5] **UKARA is a retailer database used as evidence of a UK skirmishing
defence — it is not a licence, it does not confer insurance, and it has no Irish application.**
[4][5] EVOLUTION Airsoft is an Italian brand and is **not** E&L.

## 1. Beginners & First Purchase

**1.** First three questions to a beginner. **Alan:** Establish where they'll play most (CQB, woodland or both), total budget incl. safety gear and power, and what matters most — reliability, realism, weight or upgrade potential. *Pass 2 agrees, adding: check explicitly whether the stated budget includes eye pro, battery and charger.*

**2.** Rifle you hand a first-timer most often. **Sourced/Needs Alan:** A reliable Irish-compliant **CYMA** AEG from live stock, with an **EVOLUTION M4** as the step-up. Do not name a SKU online unless Strike Arms can keep it consistently stocked. **Supersedes the pass-1 G&G/Specna answer entirely.**

**3.** Realistic all-in starting budget. **Alan:** **€300–€450** for a dependable entry AEG, battery+charger, quality BBs and rated eye pro. Don't cut corners on eye protection and the charger; cheap BBs/batteries are false economies. *Pass 2 says €280–€400 — same shape, slightly lower floor. **Alan's range stands.***

**4.** Cheap first guns you refuse to sell. **Draft v2/Needs Alan:** Avoid very cheap LPAEGs, unbranded rifles, and anything with a plastic gearbox or no parts support — weak motors, stripped plastic, poor air seal, unreliable feeding. Alan's actual shop policy still needed.

**5.** AEG vs gas vs spring for a first buy. **Alan:** **AEG is the safest first primary** — consistent, easy, economical, less cold-affected. Gas suits players who value realism and accept maintenance/weather sensitivity. Spring is limited as a primary except sniper/shotgun. Myth to correct: that gas or a sniper is automatically more powerful/effective — reliability, hop setup and consistency matter far more.

**6.** Most common battery mistake. **Alan:** Treating a LiPo like a NiMH — wrong charger, running it flat, or charging unattended. Can permanently damage the battery, swell it, damage wiring/electronics, and at worst cause a fire.

**7.** LiPo vs NiMH for a first battery. **Alan:** A **7.4V LiPo with a proper balance charger** is usually the best balance where the gun suits it. NiMH is more forgiving but bulkier with slower trigger response. Shape, connector, space, wiring, fuse and any MOSFET all matter. 11.1V is not a default beginner battery unless the gun is built for it. *Pass 2 adds: check the electronic trigger unit too.*

**8.** BB weight/quality beginners get wrong. **Alan:** Good-quality **0.25g** is a sensible start for a stock AEG. Cheap/seamed/poorly-polished BBs misfeed, jam or damage the hop. Too light drifts; too heavy for the hop drops early or feeds poorly. *Pass 2 frames it as "most Irish 1 J AEGs → 0.25g default, 0.28g if the hop lifts them and the site permits".*

**9.** Gear beyond the gun people forget. **Alan:** Full-seal rated eye protection, lower-face/dental protection, supportive grippy footwear, correct battery+charger, quality BBs, water, and a workable spare-mag plan. Check site requirements before game day. *Pass 2 adds: sites may refuse unsafe eye protection or kit that fails chrono.*

**10.** FPS/joule limits Dublin sites enforce. **Sourced:** Irish sites normally enforce a strict maximum **below 1 J for every class**, not separate higher DMR/sniper limits. Redhills requires all RIFs under 1 J and applies a lower HPA limit. Players get caught by temperature change, joule creep, inaccurate home chronos, or guns imported at UK power. [1][6]

**11.** Age/ID/purchase requirements incl. NI. **Sourced + Needs Alan:** ROI — offence to sell a realistic imitation firearm to someone **under 16**; Strike Arms may operate a stricter 18+ policy and must confirm its ID procedure. NI — UK rules: 18+, and a realistic black RIF normally requires a recognised statutory defence. [2][4][5]

**12.** Upgrade beginners rush but shouldn't. **Alan:** Internal performance parts, a stronger spring, an 11.1V battery, or an expensive optic before they understand the stock gun. Play several games, find a real limitation, then upgrade the part that addresses it.

**13.** Brands with best/worst spares support in Ireland. **Draft v2:** **CYMA** is the safest starting point — common parts, broad technician familiarity. Many **EVOLUTION** M4-pattern rifles use familiar components. **ASG** quality varies by product, and the Scorpion EVO uses more dedicated parts — check local spares availability before purchase. **Supersedes the pass-1 answer.**

**14.** Reliability of entry Specna/G&G tier. **Draft v2:** Established beginner platforms, but **Strike Arms does not sell them** and no honest page should promise a failure point or round count without Alan's repair records. Comparison brands only.

**15.** Rent first vs buy first. **Alan:** Rent when someone's unsure they'll enjoy it, hasn't handled different platforms, or doesn't know CQB vs woodland. One or two rental days prevent buying the wrong length, weight, control layout or loadout. *Pass 2 adds: also rent first if considering a sniper or gas primary.*

**16.** First thing to do to a new gun before game one. **Alan:** Charge and inspect the battery, confirm it feeds, clean the inner barrel with a dry rod, set the hop with the BB weight you'll actually use, and chrono it if possible. Don't start by opening the gearbox or adding upgrades.

**17.** Talking a beginner out of a sniper first. **Alan:** Poor first primary — low rate of fire, less forgiving, often needs expensive tuning, may face minimum-engagement rules. **Sourced addition:** in Ireland a sniper has **no legal power advantage** because it must still stay under 1 J. [1]

**18.** In-store advantage over a UK ship-in. **Alan:** Handle the gun, check size/controls, match the correct battery and accessories, test feeding/function, and get local aftercare. *Pass 2 adds: an Irish-compliant chrono check, and avoiding cross-border returns and customs uncertainty.*

**19.** Most common "broke day one" claim. **Needs Alan.** *Pass 2 lists the usual suspects — flat/unsuitable battery, badly seated mag, hop set too aggressively, wrong BBs, loose connector — but the genuine-fault-vs-user-error split must come from Strike Arms' records.*

**20.** One piece of advice for a first-year buyer. **Alan:** Buy a reliable common-platform AEG and spend the rest on rated eye pro, a proper charger, quality BBs and footwear. Don't chase max FPS or replace working internals before the gun shows a real weakness.

**12.** Krytac vs ICS vs VFC. **Draft v2:** Not Strike Arms' core brands — a long comparison dilutes topical authority. Short neutral summary only: Krytac prioritises the complete package, ICS serviceability, VFC external realism.

**13.** Acetech tracers. **Draft v2:** A compact Acetech unit, selected by barrel thread, diameter and whether muzzle-flash simulation is wanted. Tracers need tracer BBs and don't compensate for a dirty barrel, poor hop or inconsistent ammo.

**14.** Brands you avoid / repair most. **Needs Alan.**

**15.** Sniper/DMR base platforms. **Sourced/Needs Alan:** In Ireland a sniper or DMR is built for **consistency and hop performance, not extra power** — still under 1 J. [1] A common-platform CYMA base is easier to support; Alan must say which platforms Strike Arms will build and warranty.

## 3. Repairs & Tech

**1.** Diagnostic order for a dead AEG. **Alan:** Confirm safe/unloaded; test battery voltage and a known-good battery; inspect connector/wiring; check the fuse; check motor connections and any MOSFET fault; only then the motor and gearbox. Battery, connector and fuse faults are far more common than a destroyed gearbox.

**2.** V2 vs V3 gearbox failures. **Draft v2:** V2 shells are more vulnerable around the front of the cylinder window under excessive stress; V3 AK gearboxes are generally regarded as structurally robust. **Brand-specific rankings need Strike Arms' records — and Specna/G&G aren't its lines.**

**3.** Full-steel vs half-steel pistons. **Draft v2:** Internal specs change by model and production revision — don't claim a piston rack without checking that exact SKU. A partial or poorly bonded steel rack can strip when timing, AoE, spring load or shimming is wrong.

**4.** Most common self-inflicted DIY damage. **Draft v2/Needs Alan:** Poor shimming, incorrect motor height, and fitting a spring or battery the gearbox wasn't prepared for. Strike Arms' real surcharge/labour policy for correcting previous work needed.

**5.** What a correctly-set motor sounds like. **Alan:** A smooth, even whir without a sharp squeal or coarse grinding. Too high = tight/strained; too low = rough/gear-like. Leaving it wrong adds heat and accelerates pinion/bevel wear.

**6.** Buckings/nubs + combos by BB weight. **Draft v2:** For a **1 J AEG**, a well-sealed soft-to-medium bucking (~50–60°) with a correctly matched concave nub is a common start. Exact bucking must suit hop window, barrel and BB weight — publish Strike Arms' tested combinations. **Supersedes the pass-1 answer, which assumed higher-power setups.**

**7.** FPS/joule limits Irish sites enforce. **Sourced:** All AEGs, DMRs, bolt-actions and gas replicas must stay **below 1 J**; semi-lock does not create a higher class. Sites may add stricter rules — Redhills sets HPA at 0.9 J and caps BBs at 0.28g. Check the current site rulebook. [1][6]

**8.** Green gas in an Irish winter. **Draft v2:** Cold lowers gas pressure — weak cycling, poor cooldown recovery, inconsistent shots. Warming mags safely and using a suitable gas can help, but **re-chrono after**. Efficient lightweight-slide pistols cope better than heavy metal-slide designs; none are immune.

**9.** Three most common GBB gas-leak causes. **Alan:** Fill valve, output valve, and base/main mag seal. Dried/damaged O-rings, dirt and loose valves are often cheap fixes. A cracked mag body, damaged casting, corroded valve seat or unavailable proprietary part can make it uneconomical.

**10.** Lubrication by area. **Alan:** Suitable gear grease on gears/contact surfaces, appropriate cylinder lube only where needed, silicone-safe oil/grease on gas seals and O-rings. **Keep oil out of the inner barrel and hop rubber.** Common mistake: spraying silicone everywhere, or petroleum-based lube contacting rubber.

**11.** LiPo storage/charging reality. **Alan:** A correctly-sized 7.4V LiPo is usually enough; capacity by battery space/use, discharge rating only needs to meet current demand. Store at **~3.8V/cell**, use a balance charger, inspect for swelling/damage, never leave fully charged or flat for long. Over-discharging kills packs fastest.

**12.** When a MOSFET is worth it + repair cost. **Draft v2/Needs Alan:** Worthwhile for electronic protection, programmable features, or a battery setup that would otherwise stress trigger contacts. Brands, installed prices and repair costs are shop-specific.

**13.** Custom DMR build requirements. **Sourced:** An Irish DMR targets **consistency below 1 J** through air seal, stable hop, suitable barrel, correct cylinder volume and reliable semi-only electronics where the site requires it. Building near the ceiling leaves no margin for chrono variation — leave a buffer. [1]

**14.** Tightbore diameter. **Alan:** A well-made **6.03–6.05mm** barrel is a sensible general range, but bore size alone doesn't create accuracy — straightness, finish, cleanliness, stable air seal, bucking/nub, correct hop and good BBs matter more. *Pass 2 concurs.*

**15.** Guns/brands to steer beginners from. **Draft v2:** Unbranded rifles, obsolete proprietary systems, and models whose mags/hop units/electronics aren't available in Ireland. **ASG's Scorpion EVO is a strong platform but uses dedicated components** — buyers should know it isn't as universal as a basic M4 or AK.

**16.** Player self-maintenance vs leave-to-tech + interval. **Alan:** After a game: remove battery and mag, clear the chamber safely, wipe the exterior, dry moisture, clean the inner barrel with a dry non-abrasive patch. Store dry and ventilated. Don't open the gearbox, alter shimming or lube the hop rubber unless you know exactly what you're doing. For a gun used ~twice a month, inspection/service every **6–12 months** or sooner if symptoms appear. *Pass 2 prefers "book service when symptoms appear" over an invented round-count schedule — compatible.*

**17.** Realistic repair turnaround times. **Needs Alan.** Generic internet estimates would mislead.

**18.** Parts stocked vs ordered in. **Draft v2/Needs Alan:** Stock high-use universal parts plus dedicated parts for the most-sold CYMA/EVOLUTION/ASG models. Proprietary electronic units, uncommon nozzles, shells and discontinued gas-mag components create the longest delays.

**19.** Where upgrade money makes a difference. **Alan:** First: quality BBs, hop consistency, bucking/nub, fixing air-seal inconsistency. Then motor/electronics for trigger response, efficiency or control. Avoid a stronger spring or extremely tight barrel that adds stress without usable accuracy.

**20.** Telltale signs a gearbox is about to fail. **Alan:** New grinding/screeching/clicking; sudden drop or fluctuation in ROF; excessive motor/grip heat; intermittent firing; electrical smell; repeated lock-ups; double-firing; worsening misfeeds. Stop when the sound changes sharply — continuing turns a small fault into a full rebuild.

## 5. Airsoft Law in Ireland

**Pass 2 answers all 20 with citations. Every one still requires solicitor review before
publishing — a citation is not a legal opinion, and the `/airsoft-law` hub currently states no
specific rule as fact by design.**

**1.** Is airsoft legal to own here? **Sourced:** Replicas under 1 J are not classed as firearms, but realistic replicas are still regulated in public places. Most common misconception: that being under 1 J means they can be carried or displayed anywhere. [1][2][3]

**2.** The dividing line. **Sourced:** Muzzle energy **greater than 1 J**, not a single FPS number. ~328 fps applies only to a **0.20g BB** at 1 J — judge in joules with the actual test weight. [1]

**3.** How to measure and record muzzle energy. **Draft v2/Needs Alan:** Reliable chronograph, correct BB weight entered, consistent multi-shot string, record velocity **and** joules. Whether Strike Arms issues a certificate is shop policy.

**4.** Age to buy. **Sourced/Needs Alan:** Offence to sell a realistic imitation firearm to someone **under 16**; a retailer may impose a stricter 18+ policy and request photo ID. Strike Arms must confirm what it actually applies in-store and online. [2]

**5.** Buying for a minor. **Draft v2/Needs Alan:** A parent can be the purchaser, but only where intended use, supervision, storage and site-age rules are appropriate. Refuse any transaction designed to evade shop policy or that raises a safety concern.

**6.** Two-tone / bright colour. **Sourced:** Ireland does not generally require two-tone for a legal sub-1 J replica; the UK uses colour and statutory defences to regulate supply. Don't import UKARA or two-tone rules into Irish law. [2][4][5]

**7.** Wrong UK assumptions. **Sourced:** **UKARA is a retailer verification system used as evidence of a UK airsoft defence — not an Irish licence and not applicable in the Republic.** UK power limits and the UK realistic/non-realistic imitation distinction also don't replace Ireland's 1 J threshold. [1][4][5]

**8.** Transporting to a site. **Sourced:** Unloaded and out of sight in a closed case or suitable bag, battery or gas source removed where practical, travelling directly for a legitimate purpose. No tactical gear or exposed replicas on public transport or in public. [2][3]

**9.** North/South border. **Sourced/Needs Alan:** NI is under UK firearms and VCRA rules — an adult buyer may need a recognised defence for a realistic RIF, and the product must meet UK power and import requirements. Confirm products and couriers before promising cross-border delivery.

**10.** Couriers and customs. **Needs Alan + verification:** Couriers may restrict realistic replicas, batteries, green gas and CO2; customs may inspect and question power compliance or declarations. Publish only what Strike Arms' courier and Revenue/customs advice confirm.

**11.** Grey-area / restricted items. **Sourced + needs legal review:** Green gas and CO2 aren't automatically illegal, but the finished replica must stay below 1 J and carriage rules may restrict pressurised containers. Pyrotechnics, lasers, suppressor-like devices and high-power builds each raise separate questions — no blanket answer.

**12.** Private land vs licensed site. **Draft v2:** Use on an authorised site or genuinely private land with the owner's permission, a safe backstop and no risk of public alarm. A garden visible from roads or neighbours can still create serious public-order and Garda issues.

**13.** Sites and insurance. **Draft v2:** Playing at an organised, authorised, insured site doesn't change the 1 J limit, but gives controlled access, safety rules, marshals and a recognised reason for possession. Private games carry more responsibility for permission, visibility, insurance and safe operation.

**14.** Turned a sale away / Garda query. **Needs Alan.** Sensible red flags: evasive identity details, requests to exceed 1 J, talk of public display, threatening use, attempts to avoid normal paperwork.

**15.** Upgrading power at home. **Sourced:** Any upgrade taking muzzle energy **above 1 J changes the device's legal classification into a firearm**. Measure after every power-related change and leave a margin rather than tuning to exactly 1.00 J. [1]

**16.** Repairs/trade-ins — proof of ownership. **Needs Alan.** Intake should record identity, contact details, serial/identifying marks, reported condition and chrono result; Alan confirms what's required and retained.

**17.** Selling used privately. **Sourced:** Ensure the replica is below 1 J, don't sell to someone under 16, transport and hand over discreetly, document item and buyer where appropriate. **Repeated commercial dealing requires separate dealer-registration consideration.** [2]

**18.** Authoritative sources. **Sourced:** Irish Statute Book, Department of Justice guidance, An Garda Síochána or the relevant local Superintendent. Forums and retailer guides explain practice but are never the final legal source. [1][2][3]

**19.** What's changed recently. **Draft v2:** The core 1 J and public-place framework isn't new, but courier policies, site rules, retailer registration and enforcement practice change. Check anything copied from old forums against the 2009 public-place and dealer provisions, and review regularly.

**20.** One plain-English warning for the top of the law page. **Sourced:** Keep every replica below 1 J, use it only in a safe authorised setting, carry it completely out of sight with a clear lawful reason, and never display an airsoft gun in public. [1][2][3]

## 6. Buying Decisions

**1.** Beginner AEG €150–250. **Sourced/Needs Alan:** Best-supported **CYMA** AEG in current stock, or an entry **EVOLUTION** model if the budget reaches it. Don't promise a model Strike Arms can't replenish.

**2.** Case against a GBB first. **Alan:** Generally a poor first primary — expensive mags, lower capacity, temperature-sensitive gas, more cleaning/lube/seal maintenance. Suits someone who knowingly prioritises recoil, handling and realism over simplicity and all-weather consistency.

**3.** How gas falls off in Irish cold. **Draft v2:** Performance falls as temperature drops, with weaker cycling and faster cooldown obvious in **single-digit temperatures**. **No universal failure temperature** — magazine design, gas pressure and slide mass all differ, so test and chrono the exact setup. *Pass 1's "~15°C / ~10°C" figures are not corroborated — treat as removed.*

**4.** Green gas vs CO2 for a first gas pistol. **Alan:** Green gas is easier/simpler; CO2 holds pressure better in cold but runs higher pressure. Only in a gun and mag designed for it — otherwise it accelerates wear or damages valves/seals/nozzles/slides. **Every gas and temperature combination must remain under 1 J.** [1]

**5.** FPS sites enforce + DMR/single-shot catch-outs. **Sourced:** Below 1 J for AEGs, DMRs and snipers alike; sites may be stricter. **Unlike many UK sites, there is no higher Irish DMR or bolt-action power tier.** [1][6]

**6.** Why joules beats chasing FPS. **Alan:** FPS is speed only; joules is actual kinetic energy and accounts for BB weight. A gun can show lower FPS with a heavier BB while producing similar/greater energy. Chrono to the site's stated method and BB weight, and be aware of joule creep. *Pass 2: demonstrate it on the chrono with the customer's own BBs.*

**7.** Default BB weight for a stock AEG. **Draft v2:** **0.25g** is the best default for a stock Irish 1 J AEG; 0.28g may fly more steadily if the hop can lift it. The gain is wind resistance and consistency, **not extra energy**. **Supersedes the pass-1 "350–400 FPS" framing.**

**8.** Damage cheap BBs cause. **Draft v2:** Seamed, oversized, undersized or dirty BBs jam the barrel, tear the hop, misfeed and create unpredictable shots. Recommend only polished, consistent BBs from tested stock. *Pass-1's named brand list is not corroborated — drop until Alan confirms stocked lines.*

**9.** When bio BBs are genuinely needed. **Alan:** When a site or landowner specifies them. A good bio BB should perform much like a good standard BB — consistent diameter, polish, roundness, stored away from heat/moisture. Cheap bio BBs suffer the same problems as any poor ammo. *Pass 2 agrees, adding: sensible outdoors where recovery is impossible.*

**10.** Matching BB weight to gun power. **Draft v2:** At Irish power, a ~300 fps pistol may use 0.20–0.25g; most **1 J rifles** perform well on 0.25–0.28g. **A legal Irish sniper is still under 1 J and does not follow a 500 fps setup.** **The pass-1 400/500 fps examples are UK figures and must not appear on an Irish page.**

**11.** LiPo vs NiMH + C-rating. **Alan:** A 7.4V LiPo is usually the practical choice for a compatible stock AEG; NiMH is more tolerant but bulkier/less responsive. LiPo risk is mainly mishandling. Capacity must fit the stock and game length; C-rating only needs to supply the current the gun demands.

**12.** Battery mistakes that bite. **Draft v2:** Over-discharged packs, months at full charge, damaged wires, wrong connectors, batteries forced into cramped stocks. Repair cost depends on whether wiring, contacts or electronics were also damaged.

**13.** Deans vs Tamiya + MOSFET. **Alan:** Deans-style give a firmer connection and lower resistance than small Tamiya, but conversion is only worthwhile if soldered correctly and both gun and batteries are standardised. A MOSFET can protect trigger contacts and add functions but doesn't automatically mean running 11.1V. *Pass 2: the manufacturer's voltage spec still controls.*

**14.** Most common "bought online, now broken". **Draft v2/Needs Alan:** Guns arriving **above 1 J**, unsuitable batteries, poor magazine compatibility, and faults needing expensive return shipping. Actual models must come from Strike Arms' bench.

**15.** Brands that hold up vs ones you avoid. **Draft v2:** Within Strike Arms' range — **CYMA** is the strongest general-value and parts-support story, **EVOLUTION** offers more modern M4-style feature sets, **ASG** includes higher-end/licensed platforms such as the Scorpion EVO. Reliability still varies by exact model and revision.

**16.** Better gun vs better BBs/battery/hop. **Alan:** Start with a dependable base gun. After that, quality BBs, a correctly matched battery/charger and a good hop setup improve the real experience more than cosmetic parts or random gearbox upgrades.

**17.** What UK ship-in shops get wrong. **Draft v2:** UK shops may supply guns **at UK site power**, use different warranty logistics, and face cross-border VAT, customs and carriage restrictions. Buying locally gives an Irish chrono check, simpler returns and face-to-face support.

**18.** Gas/CO2 shipping and storage. **Needs Alan + verification:** Pressurised green gas and CO2 are commonly restricted by couriers and air transport even when the airsoft item itself can be sold. List only shipping methods the carrier explicitly accepts.

**19.** Realistic order of FPS upgrades. **Alan:** Establish the legal/site energy limit and measure the gun consistently. Correct air seal, hop and BB choice before power. Change the spring only when a measured adjustment is needed, then chrono again to the site's method. *Pass 2: chasing a headline FPS figure is wasted money when every Irish replica must stay under 1 J.*

**20.** One buying decision you'd stop. **Draft v2:** Stop beginners buying a sniper rifle, gas rifle or very cheap unbranded gun as their first and only primary. A reliable AEG gives more playing time and fewer immediate costs.

## 7. Gear & Loadouts

**1.** First €150 of gear, in priority order. **Alan:** Rated full-seal eye protection, lower-face/dental protection, supportive grippy boots, gloves, anti-fog and hydration, then a simple way to carry magazines. A basic chest rig or belt and one or two spare mags beat a helmet, radio, sidearm or heavy plate carrier at the start.

**2.** Eye protection that resists fogging. **Draft v2:** Full-seal polycarbonate with a recognised impact rating such as **EN166 B** where appropriate, plus anti-fog treatment or active ventilation. **Mesh-only eye protection should not be recommended** — fragments, debris and damaged BBs can pass through. *Supersedes pass 1's thermal-lens detail, which was uncited.*

**3.** Most common first-day gear mistake. **Draft v2:** Prioritising a plate carrier and accessories over footwear, eye protection, anti-fog and spare power — leaving players fogged up, slipping in mud, or finished after one battery fails.

**4.** Chest rig vs plate carrier vs battle belt. **Alan:** A chest rig is the best general start (light, ventilated, carries essentials); a battle belt suits a very light load or CQB; a plate carrier only when the player specifically wants its fit/protection/load distribution. Wrong when it adds heat, bulk and weight without carrying what's needed.

**5.** Boots for wet Irish woodland. **Draft v2:** Supportive hiking or tactical boots with strong ankle hold, deep wet-ground tread and a secure heel; waterproofing helps but breathability and good socks matter too. Avoid smooth soles and cheap zips that fail in mud.

**6.** How many mags + placement. **Alan:** Around **three to six mid-caps** depending on capacity, game length and reload availability. Primary mags on the support-hand side within natural reach; mirror for a left-handed player. Keep the rig front clear enough to crouch and go prone. *Pass 2 says three to five — Alan's range stands.*

**7.** CQB vs woodland beyond camo. **Alan:** CQB favours a shorter gun, low-profile rig, fewer bulky pouches, secure retention, a sling that keeps the gun close. Woodland carries more ammo, water and layers and a supportive two-point sling. Mid-caps are quieter/more consistent; hi-caps carry more but rattle and need winding. *Pass 2 adds full-face protection for CQB.*

**8.** Camo patterns for Irish woodland. **Draft v2:** British DPM, MTP, woodland patterns and darker green-brown combinations generally suit Irish hedgerow and mixed woodland. Solid black and pale desert patterns stand out. Best pattern changes with season and site.

**9.** Two-point vs single-point sling. **Alan:** A two-point adjustable sling is the best all-round choice — supports the gun and tightens to the body. A single-point feels quick in CQB but swings more freely. Poor adjustment is what causes the muzzle/stock to hit knees, snag gear or drag when kneeling.

**10.** Gloves/knee pads/lower-face protection. **Draft v2:** Gloves and lower-face protection are most regretted when skipped; knee pads matter on hard CQB surfaces. Hand hits are painful and an unprotected tooth can chip or break from a close BB strike.

**11.** Durable gear brands. **Needs Alan** (which brands Strike Arms actually stocks). General: cheapest failures are broken buckles, torn stitching, weak zips, and pouches that don't retain mags during movement.

**12.** Smartest layering. **Alan:** Moisture-wicking base layer, a light insulating mid-layer when needed, breathable waterproof outer that can be vented/removed. Avoid heavy cotton next to skin. Carry one compact dry/warm layer rather than wearing everything from the start.

**13.** Hydration and load-carrying. **Alan:** A bottle or bladder — whichever the player will actually clean and drink from. Carry only what that game needs, keep weight close to the body, remove duplicate tools, oversized pouches and unnecessary armour. *Pass 2 quantifies: roughly **1.5–2 litres** for a full day.*

**14.** Gear people over-buy. **Alan:** Plate carriers, helmets, radios, sidearms, optics and excessive pouches. Better spent on eye protection, boots, anti-fog, quality BBs, a spare battery and a comfortable simple rig.

**15.** Indoor CQB loadout specifics. **Alan:** Compact — shorter primary, low-profile rig/belt, fewer mags, secure pouches, lower-profile face protection, a sling that controls the gun tightly. Avoid loose straps, oversized utility pouches and anything that catches on doorways. *Pass 2: use **full-face** protection indoors.*

**16.** Dump pouches and mid-cap recovery. **Alan:** Place a dump pouch where the support hand reaches without looking (usually behind the hip on that side). Practise removing a mag, dropping it straight in and seating the replacement. Structured enough to find, closed during movement. *Pass 2 adds: mark mags with clear ownership.*

**17.** Full-seal vs mesh vs goggles; under-16 vs adult. **Sourced/Needs Alan:** **Under-18s should use full-face protection**, and sites may make it mandatory; adults should still combine full-seal eye protection with lower-face protection. Dental hits often happen around corners at close range when only glasses are worn. [7][8]

**18.** Sizing/fit mistakes ordering online. **Alan:** Measure chest and waist where the rig will sit, check the maker's adjustment range, allow for layers underneath. For a plate carrier check torso length so it doesn't sit on the stomach or clash with the belt. Buyers size from clothing labels instead of the product's actual measurements.

**19.** Single gear upgrade that transforms a struggling player's day. **Draft v2:** Better anti-fog eye protection — seeing clearly improves safety and performance every minute. Then comfortable boots or a properly adjusted sling.

**20.** Small quality-of-life items. **Alan:** Anti-fog, a spare charged battery, a speedloader, a barrel-cleaning rod, water, a microfibre cloth, electrical tape, a small basic tool and a secure dump pouch. *Pass 2 adds: dead rag, dry socks.*

## 8. Services & Business Differentiators

**1.** Three most common actual causes vs what the customer thinks. **Draft v2:** Flat or damaged battery; connector/fuse/trigger fault; motor or gearbox jam. Customers often assume the gearbox is destroyed before those simpler checks are done.

**2.** Gearbox rebuild process. **Draft v2/Needs Alan:** Diagnosis, cleaning, measurement, replacement **only of worn or unsuitable parts**, then shimming, lubrication, air-seal testing and chrono verification. **Replacing every part regardless of condition is not automatically good practice.**

**3.** Turnaround times and price ranges. **Needs Alan.** Publish ranges with clear inclusions, and note that parts availability and previous DIY damage change the quote.

**4.** Upgrade beginners waste money on vs best return. **Alan:** Waste = stronger springs, random internals or cosmetics before diagnosing the stock gun. Best return = quality BBs, correct hop setup, and a properly-fitted bucking and nub. *Pass 2 adds: an unnecessary tightbore.*

**5.** Most common DIY mistakes to undo. **Alan:** Shimming too tightly, motor height by guesswork, an unsuitable spring/battery, pinched wires, misaligned hop rubber, lost gearbox timing or small parts, and AoE changes made without understanding pickup/release. Change one thing for a measured reason and test after each step. *Pass 2 adds poor soldering.*

**6.** Brands/models that fail most vs workhorses. **Draft v2/Needs Alan:** **CYMA** has the strongest workhorse reputation within the stocked brands; **EVOLUTION** and **ASG** are more feature-rich or specialised. Real bench data needed before any failure claim.

**7.** FPS/joule limits you chrono to. **Sourced/Needs Alan:** Chrono every Irish-bound replica **below 1 J with a safety margin**. UK sites commonly use higher class-based limits that **do not apply in Ireland** — a UK-compliant gun can be illegal or unusable at an Irish site. [1][4][6]

**8.** Hop-tuning bench process. **Alan:** Clean and inspect the barrel; confirm hop chamber and barrel are centred and stable; inspect bucking lips/contact patch; fit bucking and nub without twisting/pinching; check feeding and air seal; test with the customer's BB weight; adjust for a repeatable flat flight rather than max lift; confirm consistency over the chrono. *Pass 2: finish with a chrono string and range test, not a single shot.*

**9.** Parts that fail in Irish weather. **Alan:** Cold exposes weak batteries and reduces gas pressure; damp corrodes steel parts and electrical connections; low temperature stiffens hop rubber. Wet equipment stored in a closed bag causes more damage than gear dried, ventilated and inspected promptly. *Pass 2 adds mould on soft gear.*

**10.** How you decide a MOSFET is worth fitting. **Draft v2/Needs Alan:** When the customer needs trigger-contact protection, programmable control, diagnostics or an approved higher-response battery setup. **Don't fit electronics as a status upgrade when the standard system is reliable.**

**11.** Gas vs AEG vs HPA for a first primary. **Alan:** AEG is the straightforward recommendation — consistent, widely supported, easier in Irish weather. Gas suits players prioritising recoil/realism; HPA suits experienced players willing to manage tank, line, regulator, tuning and site acceptance. **Sourced addition: Special Ops currently states HPA systems are not permitted.** [8]

**12.** What defines a good pre-loved gun. **Alan:** Should chrono consistently, feed reliably, have a sound gearbox and wiring, a working safety/selector, no structural cracks, no serious corrosion/damaged threads, and sourceable parts. Check mag fit, hop range, barrel, battery space and signs of unsafe home mods. A heavily-modified gun with no build record is a bigger risk than a clean standard example. *Pass 2 adds: **must be under 1 J**, and check serial/identifying marks.*

**13.** Warranty/aftercare vs UK ship-ins. **Needs Alan.** The differentiator should be local diagnosis, a clear test record and accessible follow-up — not a vague promise of better service.

**14.** Real cost/hassle of buying from a UK shop. **Draft v2:** Import VAT, customs administration, courier restrictions, power conversion, and expensive warranty returns. The headline UK price can be lower while total risk and aftercare cost are higher.

**15.** Why in-person advice matters. **Alan:** Face-to-face catches poor fit, excessive weight, awkward controls, the wrong battery shape/connector, incompatible mags, unsuitable eye protection and unrealistic range/power expectations. *Pass 2 adds: a gun set too close to 1 J.*

**16.** Batteries/chargers you recommend and refuse. **Draft v2/Needs Alan:** Recommend genuine balance chargers from established manufacturers and batteries matched to the rifle; refuse damaged, swollen or untraceable packs and unsafe USB-only LiPo charging. Most dangerous behaviour: unattended charging with the wrong charger.

**17.** BB quality/weight you stock. **Draft v2/Needs Alan:** Stock polished, consistent BBs; guide most 1 J AEG users to **0.25g**, testing 0.28g where suitable. Cheap ammo erases the benefit of an expensive build.

**18.** The story of the shop. **Needs Alan.** **Do not invent an anniversary, repair count or founder story for SEO.**

**19.** Questions before paying for a custom build. **Alan:** Ask the exact goal, target energy, BB weight, parts list, compatibility reasoning, expected reliability, testing method, chrono results, warranty and what happens if it misses spec. Red flags: vague promises, chasing max FPS, refusing to document parts, no test results, replacing every component unnecessarily, no clear aftercare. *Pass 2: written target **in joules**; red flags include guaranteed extreme range and no chrono printout.*

**20.** Local knowledge you give customers. **Sourced/Needs Alan:** Recommend by play style and distance — **Khaos** and **Fingal** north of Dublin, **Special Ops** in Wicklow, **Redhills** in Kildare — checking each current booking page before publication. Include rentals, age rules, chrono limits and beginner-friendliness. [6][7][8][9]
