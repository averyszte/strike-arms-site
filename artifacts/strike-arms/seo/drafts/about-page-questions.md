# /about — what Alan needs to answer

`/about` is **live** in a lean form, built only from answers Alan has already given: why in-person
advice catches what a product page cannot, the in-store advantage over ordering in, and the
workshop. It stands on its own and states nothing invented.

What it is missing is the part that makes an about page worth reading — **the story**. That is
§8.18 and Appendix A2/A9/A10 in [alan-interview-answers.md](../alan-interview-answers.md), all
marked *Needs Alan*.

**Do not invent any of this.** Not an anniversary, not a repair count, not a founder narrative,
not a years-of-experience figure. An about page caught embellishing is worse than a short one.

---

## The five questions

**1. How did you get into airsoft, and how did that become a shop?**
The actual sequence — player first, tech first, something else? What was the moment you decided to
sell rather than just fix? Specific and awkward beats polished; the detail people remember is
usually the one that sounds too small to include.

**2. How many years have you been teching, and how long has Strike Arms existed?**
Two separate numbers. Do not merge personal experience with company history, and do not round up —
if it is six years say six. One verified number does more work than three vague ones.

**3. Why a physical shop in Swords, rather than online only?**
The commercial reasoning. What can you do from a unit with a bench in it that a website cannot?
This is the spine of the whole page, because it is also the answer to "why not just buy from the UK".

**4. Who does the technical work, and what stands behind it?**
Is it you, or a team? What training, supervised experience or manufacturer qualifications exist?
**Only claim a certification that can be documented.** "Fifteen years on the bench" is a stronger
and safer claim than an implied credential.

**5. What is your pricing position against UK shops — honestly?**
Where can you match them, and where can you not? The credible version names the cases where you are
more expensive and explains what the customer gets for it. Claiming you are always cheaper is both
untrue and easy to disprove in thirty seconds.
