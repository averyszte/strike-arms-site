# Alan Call — Question Bank

Don't read this list at him. Pick 10–15 that fit the natural flow. The "why" notes are for you, not to say out loud.

---

## Opening (warm-up, get him talking)

- "Walk me through how the shop runs today, day-to-day."
  *Why: gives context for everything else. Let him lead — listen for pain points he mentions unprompted.*

- "What's been working well with how you sell now, and what's been the most annoying part?"
  *Why: pain points dictate priorities better than any feature list. The thing he hates is what the site has to solve first.*

---

## Current Operations

- "How do customers buy from you right now?" (Facebook DMs? Phone? Walk-in? Email? Existing site?)
  *Why: tells you what to replace or import, and what's the most natural transition to online.*

- "Do you have a POS system in the shop?"
  *Why: if yes, inventory may need to sync. If no, your system becomes the single source of truth from day one.*

- "How do you track stock right now — spreadsheet, gut feel, the POS?"
  *Why: tells you what data exists, what format it's in, how accurate it is, and how much migration work is realistic.*

- "Do you take phone orders or special orders?"
  *Why: confirms whether manual order entry in admin is needed from day one.*

---

## People Using the Admin

- "Apart from you, who else would need to use the admin dashboard?"
  *Why: tells you if multi-staff accounts and role permissions are needed in v1, or if single-user is fine.*

- "If there's staff, do you want them to see everything, or only certain things — like orders but not the sales reports?"
  *Why: dictates whether a permissions system is in scope.*

---

## Orders & Pickup

- "What happens today when an order needs to be ready for a customer?"
  *Why: defines the staff workflow you're automating. Listen for how many steps are currently involved.*

- "When a customer comes to pick up, what's your process?"
  *Why: defines the in-store pickup flow. ID check? Order number? Anything else?*

- "How often do customers not show up for pickups, and what do you do when that happens?"
  *Why: tells you if an automated no-show workflow is needed or if it's rare enough to handle manually.*

- "How do you handle returns and refunds today?"
  *Why: shapes the refund flow. Some shops give store credit by default; some always refund to original payment.*

- "Would you want customers to pick a specific pickup time slot, or just 'any time during open hours'?"
  *Why: slots add real complexity. Most local shops are fine without them — get his preference early.*

---

## Inventory & Products

- "How often does your inventory change — daily, weekly?"
  *Why: high-volume churn means bulk-edit tools are needed. Low churn means a simple single-product form is fine.*

- "Do you ever run sales or promotions? What format — percentage off, fixed price, something else?"
  *Why: confirms whether discount codes are launch-critical or can wait for Tier 2.*

- "When a new product comes in, how would you want to add it to the site — photo and a few fields, or bulk-import from supplier files?"
  *Why: dictates whether CSV import is needed or a single-product form is enough.*

- "Who takes the product photos — you, the suppliers, or someone else?"
  *Why: affects the image upload workflow. Phone photos → single upload with auto-resize. Supplier assets → bulk upload might be needed.*

- "Do products vary by colour, size, or any option — or is each thing its own listing?"
  *Why: this is the variants question. Airsoft guns generally aren't variant-heavy, but gear and accessories might be. Variants are a significant data model decision — important to know now.*

---

## Customers & Inquiries

- "Do customers contact you mostly by phone, email, social, or in-store?"
  *Why: confirms what contact channels the site needs to surface prominently.*

- "How do you keep track of customers today — a list, a spreadsheet, or mostly in your head?"
  *Why: tells you if a customer management feature is essential or just nice-to-have.*

- "Would you want to see who your repeat customers are, or total spend per customer?"
  *Why: opens the door for basic customer-value reporting. Some owners care, some don't.*

---

## Reporting & Business View

- "What does your accountant need from you each month?"
  *Why: tells you what the sales export needs to include — VAT breakdown, by-product totals, refunds, etc.*

- "Are there any numbers you'd want to see at a glance daily — like 'how much did I make today' or 'what's selling well'?"
  *Why: shapes the admin dashboard home screen.*

---

## Site Features

- "Do you want customers to create accounts, or would you prefer a guest checkout for now?"
  *Why: accounts add complexity (password reset, order history UI, GDPR exposure). Guest checkout is simpler for v1.*

- "For services like repairs and upgrades — do you want customers to be able to book online, or is it phone/walk-in only?"
  *Why: online booking is a separate feature with its own complexity. Worth scoping separately.*

- "Is there anything about the current site you definitely want to keep?"
  *Why: avoids accidentally removing something he's attached to.*

---

## Catch-All & Wish List

- "If you could wave a magic wand and have the site do anything for the shop, what would it be?"
  *Why: opens up dreaming. He might mention something unexpected — loyalty program, members-only gear drops, repair job tracking.*

- "Is there anything you've seen on other shops — UK airsoft sites, international ones — that you really liked?"
  *Why: gives reference points. 'Like Patrol Base' or 'like Evike' tells you a lot about expectations.*

- "What's a deal-breaker for you? What would have to be true for you to feel comfortable using this every day?"
  *Why: surfaces hidden requirements — often something like 'I need to be able to undo mistakes' or 'I need to print from it'.*

---

## Closing

- "Anything else on your mind that I haven't asked about?"
  *Standard close — the most important thing is often the last thing said.*

- "When were you thinking of launching, roughly?"
  *Why: sets the timeline, affects scope decisions, and your own scheduling.*

---

## How to Run the Call

- Don't ask all of them. Pick 10–15, let the conversation flow naturally.
- Take notes — open a Google Doc or notepad before the call. Say upfront: "Mind if I take notes?"
- Park technical questions — if he asks about tech, deflect: "I'll handle the tech side, let's keep this on what you need it to do."
- Repeat back what you hear — "So to confirm: you're currently taking orders by phone, you want the admin to flag stale pickups, and you don't currently run promotions but might want to. Yeah?" Confirms you've listened, catches misunderstandings early.
- End with next steps — "I'll send a quick summary of what we discussed and what I'm proposing to include. Then I'll get you a clear scope and timeline for launch."
